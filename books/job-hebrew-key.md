# Job 1-4: Every Word of the Volume, to Its Hebrew

*Companion to `books/job.md`. Word for word: every English word this volume
prints, in its printed order, with the Hebrew word that renders it and the
Strong's number that tags it (OpenScriptures Hebrew Bible, MorphHB). A
reference file, not law (5.1). Generated 26 October 2026.*

**How to read it.**

- The volume prints its translation without verse numbers. The verse divisions
  below are the received ones, cut by hand where the tagged text cuts. The cuts
  were machine-checked to consume the volume's words exactly, in order, none
  missing and none repeated.
- A dash in the Hebrew column means the English word has no Hebrew word of its
  own: the article, an auxiliary, a word absorbed into a Hebrew ending.
  English and Hebrew do not match one to one, and the table shows where they
  do not.
- One Hebrew word often serves several English words ("his name" = שמו). The
  word is printed at each of the words it serves.
- A row marked *without English* at a verse's end is a Hebrew word the volume
  renders by no word of its own: the object marker את, an oath particle, a
  connective the English leaves off. It is shown, not hidden.
- Hebrew is consonantal. The pointed forms, word by word, live in
  `books/job-strongs.md`.
- A slash divides a prefixed particle from its word: `ב/` in, `ו/` and, `ה/`
  the, `כ/` like, `ל/` to, `מ/` from, and the interrogative ה.
- Rows marked ◆ print the bless-euphemism as written. The sense is "curse",
  and the volume's notes carry it (1:5, 1:11, 2:5, 2:9).
- The numbers are concordance handles, not renderings. Where the Code's tables
  mandate a rendering (עֶבֶד = slave, הַשָּׂטָן = the accuser, and the rest),
  the volume follows the Code, not the gloss a number is filed under. The
  alignment itself was done by hand and then machine-verified end to end: the
  Hebrew letter stream against the tagged text, the English tokens against
  `books/job.md`, and every number against the same tagged text.
