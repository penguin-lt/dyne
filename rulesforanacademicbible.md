# RULES FOR AN ACADEMIC BIBLE

*The law of the Dyne translation. One file, no second rule document. Simplified 26 October 2026:
the same rulings, a plainer container. Cite a Part by its title, not its number — numbers drift,
titles do not.*

---

# PART 1: WHAT THIS IS

## 1.1 The work

An academic edition for historical-critical study. It reports what the ancient authors wrote, in
ordinary modern English, without fear of the institution and without hatred of the believer.

Three sentences govern every decision:

> **Push everything modern. We are not a museum. We are a forensic historical document.**
> **We do not sanitize the ancient physiology.**
> **The text belongs to the reader's own sovereign judgment.**

## 1.2 Order of authority

1. This file.
2. The Eight Laws (Part 3), supreme over everything except the Ambiguity Rule where a source text
   is genuinely ambiguous.
3. The Ambiguity Rule (Part 6), the only Part that can suspend a rendering mandated elsewhere.
4. The Jewish Mandate (1.4), which ranks with the Laws.
5. The operator's standing instructions (Part 8), which cannot be overridden by inference. Where
   one departs from a Law, the departure is deliberate.
6. The terminology tables in 4.2, the first resort for any single word.

## 1.3 The penalty clause

A translation that violates these laws is not a translation. It is a theological pamphlet or a
counterfeit: a counterfeit if it hides that God commanded the slaughter of nursing infants, if it
turns enslaved people into "bondservants", if it injects later dogma into first-century Greek, if
it invents permissive language to protect God from his own text, or if it softens the viscera of
grief into an abstraction.

## 1.4 The Jewish Mandate

These are Jewish texts, and the translation is answerable to the world that produced them.

1. No sanitizing of the ancient physiology. The organs of grief, the viscera, the genitals and the
   dung render as the sources render them.
2. No nineteenth-century gentility.
3. No fourth-century Greek categories imposed on first-century Jewish monotheism. *Ousia* and
   *hypostasis* are later apparatus. They may appear in a note as reception history and may never
   be required to make a sentence of the text coherent.
4. The Second Temple divine realm is reported, not flattened: the divine council, the Angel of the
   Lord, the Logos.
5. Where two renderings are equally defensible, prefer the one that can be read without later
   doctrinal apparatus.

**What the Mandate does not license.** It is not a mandate for a low Christology. It forbids the
translation from *requiring* later doctrine. It does not forbid the text from saying more than the
Mandate expected, and where the text does, the text wins.

## 1.5 House style, which binds this file too

No semicolons anywhere, in any file. No Victorian archaism. No British spellings. Where a note
quotes an older English version, the quotation keeps that version's wording and the note says so.

# PART 2: WHO DOES WHAT

## 2.1 The two roles

**Every rule has one owner at the moment it runs.** Where a rule must run at two points of the
work, it is two duties, one per phase, and the ownership table (2.8) names each. A translator works
from the source toward a text. An auditor works from the finished text backward toward the rules.

1. **Neither list is handed to the other role.** A translator given the audit criteria writes to
   the check instead of to the source. An auditor given the translator's aims grades the intention
   instead of the artifact.
2. **The split is by role, not by difficulty.** A rule goes to the auditor when only an outside
   look at the finished text can know whether it was obeyed.
3. **The auditor does not translate and the translator does not audit.** One person may hold both
   roles in a project, never on the same volume. The reviewer who signs a volume is never its
   author.
4. **This is law.** Where the two lists conflict, the conflict is a defect in this Part and is
   resolved in 2.8, never by whichever role is speaking.

## 2.2 The order of operations

| Step | Who | In | Out |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Draft | translator | the specification and the source | the running text and the notes, nothing else |
| 2. Audit | a different pass | the draft, unchanged | a verdict per clause |
| 3. Fix | translator | the fail list | a revised draft |
| 4. Re-audit | step 2 again | every clause, not only the failed ones | a verdict per clause |
| 5. Sign | a reviewer who is not the author | the audited volume | a signature, which is not a check |

A fix for one Law can break another, so the re-audit is the whole list.

## 2.3 The authorship label

Whether a text was made by a person or a model is an audit question, assigned after translation.

1. The translator does not declare it. Nothing written during drafting may state or imply whether
   the work is human or machine.
2. The audit assigns it from evidence, in three states, never two: **verified human**,
   **verified model**, **undetermined**.
3. **Undetermined is the default and not a failure.** Guessing human is flattery, guessing model is
   an accusation, and both are unsupported claims standing where a verdict belongs.
4. The label is not a quality claim. A machine-made volume that passes is compliant. A human-made
   volume that fails is dead.
5. Evidence is only what is in the artifact and can be named: a dated signature, a decision log in
   a named hand, a transcript, a version history, a declaration from the operator. **An impression
   is not evidence.**
6. It is recorded once, in the decision log, with the evidence beside it. If the evidence
   disappears, the label is withdrawn to **undetermined** in a dated line.

## 2.4 The translator's list

**Fourteen duties, done to the source, before the text exists in finished form.**

1. **Read the source as the source.** Establish the base text and its edition, and know which
   witnesses are in the room, before writing.
2. **Render the word, not the tradition of the word.** The terminology tables are statute. Where a
   table is silent, the word's range in the source decides, and the decision is logged once.
3. **Print the most literal English the grammar allows.** Where more than one reading is live and
   they carry different freight, do not choose: the text prints the literal rendering and the note
   carries the range. Part 6.
4. **Run the substitution control.** Put an uncontested name in place of the contested one. If the
   rendering changes, it was doctrine and not grammar.
5. **Refuse the typography temptation.** Capitals, small caps, italics, scare quotes (4.4, item 7).
6. **Keep the anatomy an anatomy.** Organs are organs, victims are the victims of named agents, and
   the agent of a verb is the agent the source names. Laws II, III and V are obeyed in the act of
   writing the clause or not at all.
7. **Do not harmonize.** Where two voices disagree, both stand. Where a seam is visible, the seam
   is printed. Law VI.
8. **Nothing is sanitized.** Anatomy, violence, sexual violence, slavery, the cost to the victim.
   It prints.
9. **Use the names this Code mandates.** Standard English forms, Jahve as 4.2 states it, and the
   three reversals of 4.3. A form printed against 4.3 in either direction is a defect.
10. **Modern register as you write.** No semicolon, no "behold", no archaic verb, no inverted
    negation, "who" for persons and "that" for things. Part 4.
11. **Write every note head from the text as printed, never from the intention.** The head is
    written after its verse is final.
12. **Disclose nothing about yourself.** No statement of origin, no ledger, no self-assessment, no
    claim that a rule was followed. 2.3 governs.
13. **List the verses before rendering, and the verses after.** Give the audit both lists. The
    audit counts and compares them. The translator produces evidence and certifies nothing.
14. **Deliver the text and stop.** Anything the translator writes about the quality of its own
    output is discarded unread. If the translator believes a rule is wrong, the translator says so
    and stops. It does not print against the table and explain in a note.

If one of these cannot be answered, say which and why, and let the operator decide.

## 2.5 Scope and counting, which both roles run

1. **Before writing, check whether the book is already done.** If it is, read the existing version
   first. Its rulings bind.
2. **If a draft differs from what exists, stop and ask.** A change may be proposed and may not be
   made silently. Two versions of one book with different text is the worst outcome available.
3. **If the operator names a book already complete, confirm the task.** A fresh edition, a
   revision, and a replacement are three jobs with three acceptance criteria.
4. **The counting rule.** Every number in a disclosure block is a counted number: the note heads,
   the verses, the citations. If you cannot count, write "not counted" and say why. An estimated
   number is an invented one.

## 2.6 The auditor's list

**Eleven duties, done to a finished text from outside it.**

1. **Take the artifact unchanged.** A repair made during an audit voids the audit of that passage.
2. **Run the mechanical gates first and record what they printed.** A pass read from memory is not
   a pass.
3. **Give every clause one of four verdicts: PASS, FAIL, MANUAL, N/A.** MANUAL is not a pass. It
   means no detector exists and a person must read it.
4. **Check the note heads against the rendered text, not against the intention.**
5. **Check every factual claim in every note against a named source** — a hapax, an attribution, a
   siglum, a lexical gloss.
6. **Re-read every note for the claim that sounds certain and was never checked.** A number is a
   claim. A superlative is a claim. "The volume prints X" is a claim about a file, and the file is
   right there.
7. **Check provenance, not only conformity.** A volume can pass every style gate while carrying
   another book's text.
8. **Write the ledger from the audit's own output,** not from memory or the translator's account.
9. **Check statements of correction against the artifact.** Every claim that something was fixed
   names the file and the verse.
10. **Assign the authorship label from evidence, or decline to.** 2.3.
11. **Report, and do not fix.** Corrections go back to the translator, and the revised text comes
    back for a full re-audit.

**The auditor may never** accept a translator's self-report as evidence, or pass a clause because
the text reads well, because the author is reputable, or because a model would not have made that
mistake.

## 2.7 What binds both roles

1. No semicolons, in any file, in any output.
2. Modern register, no archaism, no inverted negation, no British spellings.
3. The terminology tables are statute for every rendering, spoken or printed.
4. No fabrication of form, fact, correction or self-report (7.2).
5. An uncited assertion about a scholar is a fabrication.
6. A note quoting an older version keeps that version's wording.
7. **When in doubt, say a thing is unchecked.** No rule forbids this sentence.

## 2.8 Who owns which rule

| Rule | Owner |
|---|---|
| A rendering the tables do not cover | Translator |
| The literal rendering at a live ambiguity | Translator |
| The anatomy, the agent, the victim | Translator |
| The preserved contradiction and the printed seam | Translator |
| Names and geography in running text | Translator |
| The form of a note head | Translator |
| The content of the decision log | Translator |
| A repair, once the audit has reported | Translator |
| The translator's list (2.4) | Translator |
| The specification block | Translator fills it, auditor rejects blanks |
| Whether a note head matches the verse | Auditor |
| Every fact, number and superlative in every note | Auditor |
| The gates, the five failures, the poison suite, the verdicts | Auditor |
| The honesty ledger and the authorship label | Auditor |
| Scope discipline and the counting rule (2.5) | Both |
| The badge and the reviewer's signature | Neither |

# PART 3: THE EIGHT LAWS

## Law I: Textual Pluriformity

**There is no single pristine original text.** Where the received text carries a later theological
censorship, the text follows the older witness and the received reading goes in the note. **But age
is not the test. The direction of the corruption is the test.**

| Case | Test | What is printed |
|---|---|---|
| Censorship | The softer reading adds a doctrine, a dignity or a scruple, and there is no motive for the reverse change | The harder, earlier reading. The received text in the note |
| Documented correction | The tradition preserved both the reading and the fact that it changed it | The earlier reading at full strength. The received text in the note, named as a correction |
| True variant | Neither reading explains the other, and the difference is not doctrinal | The better-attested reading, the other in the note |

The second case is the first case with better evidence: a scribal correction is a censorship the
censors admitted. The law does not license reverse snobbery — every old reading is not thereby
early. That is a case-by-case textual judgment, recorded in the note.

**Worked case (Habakkuk 1:12).** The received text reads *lo namut*, "we shall not die". The
tradition records *lo tamut*, "you shall not die", addressed to God, as the reading the scribes
replaced, because associating death with God was intolerable to them. The Greek version already
reads "we shall not die", which shows the corruption was already in place when the Greek was made:
early, not original. Whether any older witness carries "you shall not die" is a question the note
answers with named witnesses or marks unconfirmed, because a siglum is never attached to a scroll
whose contents have not been checked (4.4, item 5). The parallelism settles it: "from everlasting"
is answered by "you shall not die", while "we shall not die" breaks the couplet. The text prints
*lo tamut* and the note records the received reading.

## Law II: Unvarnished Anatomy

**We do not sanitize the ancient physiology.** No organ is replaced by an abstraction, and no
visceral metaphor is psychologized.

| Hebrew | Rendering | Forbidden |
|---|---|---|
| מֵעַי | **bowels** | "inner parts", "inmost being", "heart", "stomach" |
| כָּבֵד | **liver** | "soul", "vital force" |
| כְּלָיוֹת | **kidneys** | "reins" |
| חֲלָצַיִם | **loins** | "waist" where the idiom is sexual or generative |
| בֶּטֶן | **belly / womb** | "stomach", "tummy" |
| מְרֵרָה | **gall** | "bitterness" where the organ is named |
| טִפֻּחִים | **dandled** | "nursed", "born healthy" |
| פָּתָה | **seduce** | any softening verb |
| רַגְלַיִם | **genitals** where the euphemism requires it | "feet" left opaque |
| פַּרְשְׁדֹנָה | **fecal filth** | "the dagger came out behind" |

**The carve-out, in force.** "Bowels" is the chosen word wherever the Hebrew names the organ, and
the anatomy of grief is exempt from every register sweep. "Entrails" is reserved for animals.

*Test case.* Judges 3:22 states that the fecal filth burst from the wound. Sanitizing it erases the
author's deliberate humiliation of Moab's king.

## Law III: Direct Causation

**God is responsible for what the text says he does.** No permissive language protects God's
reputation. If the text says God hardened a heart, the translation does not say he "allowed" it. If
it says God incited a census, the translation does not substitute an adversary. **God is the agent
of his own violence.** רָע / רָעָה is "evil" — never "disaster", "harm" or "calamity" where God
brings or creates it, even where that sounds harsh. The harshness is the text.

*Worked case.* Jonah 1:4 reads "hurled a great wind", not "sent out a great wind".

## Law IV: Philological Absolutism

**Words mean what they meant to their ancient authors, not what later dogma wants them to mean.**

- עַלְמָה is "young woman of childbearing age", not "virgin". "Virgin" is בְּתוּלָה.
- נֶפֶשׁ is "life, throat, living being", not an immortal soul. Animals have it too.
- עֶבֶד / δοῦλος is **slave**, property, in chattel, market and household contexts. Never
  "bondservant".
- אֱמוּנָה is "faithfulness, steadfast loyalty", not cognitive belief.
- שְׁאוֹל, ᾅδης, γέεννα and Τάρταρος are four distinct realms. None is the medieval Hell.
- רוּחַ אֱלֹהִים at Genesis 1:2 is "a mighty wind" or "divine breath", not the Holy Spirit of
  fourth-century trinitarianism.

## Law V: The Bleeding Victim

**The text is translated from the perspective of the crushed, not the powerful.**

- חֵרֶם is **total extermination, ritual slaughter under taboo**, not "devotion to the Lord".
- The Amalekite infants of 1 Samuel 15 are nursing babies at the breast, not "vessels of wrath".
- The enslaved girl of Leviticus 25 is inherited human property, not a "servant".
- Abishag's requisition (1 Kings 1:1–4) is coercive state kidnapping, not an "honored palliative
  nurse".
- Exodus 21:20–21 prints the statute with legal clarity: beating a slave to death carries no
  punishment if the victim lingers a day or two, "for he is his property".

No euphemism for torture, infant slaughter or sexual violence.

## Law VI: Preserved Contradiction

**The Bible contradicts itself, and the translation lets the contradiction stand.**

- 2 Samuel 24:1 says God incited the census. 1 Chronicles 21:1 says an adversary did. Both print
  raw.
- 2 Kings 24:8 gives a king as eighteen, 2 Chronicles 36:9 as eight. The discrepancy stands.
- The resurrection accounts print as four, never synchronized.
- The terrified, forsaken Jesus of Mark is never infected with the serene Logos of John.

The notes highlight the seams and do not referee them. Source criticism may be named descriptively
in a note. The translator reports the seam and does not rule on it.

## Law VII: Literary Formatting

**The ancient authors wrote literature. Chapter and verse numbers were medieval inventions.**

- Poetry is set as stanzas, keeping the parallelism. Acrostics are labeled by their letters, every
  letter, in order.
- Narrative and dialogue are prose, with speeches standing out.
- Law collections are legal documents, with epigraphic damage preserved.
- Inscriptions keep their line numbers.
- Verse numbers appear only in note heads and appendices.

## Law VIII: Sovereign Objectivity

**Written without fear of the institution and without hatred of the believer.** It does not insert
"[other]" into a verse, or "Jehovah" into the Greek, or change a verse to enforce male domination,
or put scare quotes around a biblical figure to police the reader's belief. A reading is not
improved by being embarrassing to a later council and not worsened by being convenient to one. The
evidence decides.

# PART 4: HOW TO RENDER

The ancient writers used the everyday speech of their own street. Register must never cost the text
its shock.

## 4.1 The register rules

**Rule 1. Negative inversion is abolished.** "Walks not" is not English: "does not walk". With a
perfect tense, "have not spoken", never "do not have spoken".

**Rule 2. "Who" for persons, "that" for things.** "That" survives for things and fused relatives
over things ("all that he had").

**Rule 3. הִנֵּה is a deictic pointer**, rendered by its function in the scene. "Behold" is banned
outright. Sudden action takes "suddenly" or "just then". A pointed-out thing takes "and there was".
A shock takes "Look!"

**Rule 4. Lexical modernization.**

|---|---|---|
| fowl | bird, birds | plural after "every" |
| beast | animal, animals | "beast" survives only in fixed idiom |
| slay, slew, slain | kill, killed | |
| smite, smote, smitten | strike, struck | |
| corn | grain | |
| ass, asses | donkey, donkeys | |
| lest | so that ... not | |
| save (preposition) | except | the verb is untouched |
| for ever | forever | |
| loving-kindness | steadfast love | |
| wherewithal, wherewith | how, with which | |
| goodly, comely | fine, fitting | |
| damsel, maiden, maid | young woman, servant girl | |

| twain | two | |
| abide | stay | |
| tarry | wait | |
| whatsoever, whoso | whatever, whoever | |
| begat, begotten | fathered | or "became the father of" |
| it came to pass | it happened | |

The "forth" compounds render by sense: motion verbs take "out", creation verbs take their own verbs
— the earth **sprouted** grass, the land **yielded**, a woman **gave birth**.

**Rule 5. What is not modernized.** "Seed", "womb", "loins", "cattle", "vine", "incense",
"Sabbath", "Sheol", and every proper name stay. The target is Victorian syntax and vocabulary, not
the Bible's own conceptual world. The vocative "O" is retained. The anatomy carve-out of Law II
overrides every sweep.

**Rule 6. Note heads follow the text.** Any change to the translation invalidates every head that
quotes it. Heads are re-derived after every pass, never patched.

**Rule 7. The received text in the text, the versions in the note.** Where the received Hebrew and
a daughter version differ and the difference is load-bearing for later theology, the text prints
the Hebrew and the note records the version. The alternative has been practiced for centuries — a
Greek reading promoted, a Jewish reading demoted, the reader never told. Test case: Psalms 22:16,
Hebrew *ka'ari* "like a lion", Greek "they pierced". The English tradition printed the Greek as
though it were the Hebrew. Not here.

## 4.3 Names are standard English

Personal and place names take their standard English forms — Jesus, Moses, David, Jacob, Peter,
Paul, Mary, Jerusalem, Egypt, Babylon, Canaan, Zion, Galilee. **The divine name is Jahve
throughout.** A Semitic form in running text is a defect. The three reversals are the whole list of
exceptions:

- **Jacob** is the patriarch, **James** the apostle.
- **Judah** is the tribe, **Judea** the province, **Judas** the man — the default in 1 and 2
  Maccabees.
- **Israel** stays English. It is the people's own self-designation.

Modern scholars and publishers are cited under their own names. Era terms stay: Jewish,
Israelite, Pauline, Johannine, Maccabean, Hasmonean.

## 4.4 The prohibitions

1. **No word that post-dates the sources** as a translation equivalent — *Trinity, incarnation,
   eucharist, sacrament, orthodox, catholic, purgatory, church* for assembly, *mass, liturgy*.
   They appear only in historical notes, marked as later categories.
2. **No interpretive paraphrase** introduced to fix theology. The strangeness is preserved and
   discussed, not smoothed.
3. **No semicolons**, in any volume or any project file.
4. **No British spellings.**
5. **No confirmed siglum on an unconfirmed manuscript.** A real siglum on the wrong scroll is the
   worst defect there is, because every element of it is true except the relation.
6. **No bracketed modifier and no scare quote**, ever, on the ground that the reader needs
   steering.
7. **No interpretive typography.** No italic, small cap or enlarged letter may signal a reading the
   plain words do not carry. "I am" is never printed "I AM". The manuscripts have no capitals, so
   every capital in a modern translation is an editorial decision, and a lawful capital has exactly
   two uses: a proper name, and a fixed title the language treats as one — "the Word", "Lord" as
   address, "Messiah", "Rock", "the Adversary", "the one and only Son". A capital may never settle
   an open ambiguity: "god" prints lower case at John 1:18 and "son" lower case at Psalms 2:12.
   The same word in the same sense prints the same way in every verse, **unless a standing
   instruction fixes the printing** (Part 8, items 30 to 32).

# PART 5: TEXT AND NOTES

## 5.1 The base text

**This Code names no translation, edition, manuscript or scholar as authoritative.** The base
translation, the critical editions, the comparison versions and the reference works are declared
once, in the volume's specification block, with edition and year. The specification is part of the
volume and is audited with it.

**What the Code requires of the declaration.**

1. The base is a literal, public-domain translation, and the revision is disciplined and
   traceable, not a fresh paraphrase.
2. Every verse is re-checked against the declared critical edition, and changed wherever the base
   followed a received text or an outdated critical judgment.
3. The comparison versions are public domain in the operator's jurisdiction, each named with its
   edition.
4. A descriptive reference work is not an authority. Commentaries, lexicons and encyclopedias are
   cited descriptively, never as a base.
5. None of this is imported into the running text. A version is cited in a note, never printed as
   though it were the source.

## 5.2 The specification block

**Fill it in and keep it at the top of the working file. A blank field means the volume is not
ready to be written, and is a failure of the audit.**

```
JOB SPECIFICATION
  Volume:             <book, chapter range>
  Base translation:   <author, title, year, public domain where>
  Textual base OT:    <edition, or the named manuscript>
  Textual base NT:    <edition>
  Witnesses:          <list, or "primary only">
  Lexicon Hebrew:     <edition>
  Lexicon Greek:      <edition>
  Grammar:            <edition>
  Note density:       <textual / crux / full apparatus>
  Appendix:           <yes, no, or which>
  Mode:               <code and file tools / conversation only>
  Register ceiling:   4.1, vocative O retained, anatomy carve-out ON
```

Two notes. **When two reference works disagree on a contested gloss, the note names both and says
so** — it does not silently take the one that suits the rendering. **If the operator cannot consult
a critical apparatus at all, the notes say so**: a note reporting a variant without saying where it
is attested is quoting somebody else's summary, and is written as a report of a report.

## 5.3 The textual policy

1. **The tetragrammaton never appears in the New Testament, in any form.** No Greek manuscript has
   it there. "Jehovah" is barred everywhere.
2. **The Comma Johanneum (1 John 5:7–8)** is excised as a late Latin interpolation.
3. **The Longer Ending of Mark (16:9–20) and the Pericope Adulterae (John 7:53–8:11)** are
   demarcated in the text as later additions.
4. **Scribal corrections such as the tiqqunei soferim** are documented in the notes where they
   occur (Genesis 18:22, Job 7:20, Zechariah 2:8, Habakkuk 1:12), with both readings and the
   direction of the change. **The earlier reading prints**, by Law I, second case.
5. **No bracketed dogmatic modifiers, no scare quotes, no sectarian repair.**
6. **Greek κύριος in the Maccabees is "the Lord"**, a standing choice scoped to the Greek books,
   with the note that a lost Hebrew original may have carried the tetragrammaton behind it.

## 5.4 How a volume is built

The base translation is scaffolding, not a second source. It supplies the starting wording and the
verse order, and every reading is then re-derived from the source text. Where base and source
disagree the source wins.

1. Take the declared base and stabilize the structure.
2. Modernize the English to 4.1.
3. Re-check every verse against the declared critical edition.
4. Apply the terminology tables.
5. Write the notes.
6. Deliver as `books/<name>.md`.
7. Set the literary layout per Law VII.
8. Run the audit of Part 7.

**A volume contains, in this order:** title and standing subtitle, the base-text block, an
introduction (genre, date, language, provenience, text and witnesses, register), the translation,
the notes, sources with licenses, and an appendix where the book calls for one.

## 5.5 The notes

- **A note head carries the citation and a verbatim quotation of the text as printed:**
  `**12:3 "the exact words of the verse"**`, matching character for character after normalization.
- **A note explains and never preaches.** It documents the variant, names the crux, reports what
  the tradition did with it, and leaves the verdict to the reader.
- **A note gives, in this order and only where there is something to say:** the text as printed,
  the witnesses where they differ, the versions where the reading bears on the verse, the
  alternative renderings and why they were taken or not, the word's range, the setting. An empty
  field means nothing to report.
- **Uncertainty is flagged, never hidden** inside smooth English.
- **No apologetic rationalization, no "the church teaches".** A note reports a book's theology as
  the ancient author's belief, never as counsel to the reader.

## 5.6 The text itself

No chapter or verse numbers in the running text. Prose in paragraphs, speeches standing out,
poetry in stanzas, a superscription's numbering convention stated once per volume.

# PART 6: THE AMBIGUITY RULE

## 6.1 The rule

Where the grammar leaves more than one reading live and the readings carry different theological
freight, **the translation prints the most literal English the grammar allows and the note carries
the full range with the argument for each reading.** The translator does not declare a winner in
the text. **A printing rule is not a verdict:** a ruling that fixes how a verse prints decides
nothing about which reading is true, and the note carries the range even where the printing is
fixed by instruction.

A translator who resolves a genuine ambiguity in the direction his own tradition prefers is doing
what the sectarian translator does. The only difference is which institution is served.

**It requires.** The literal rendering in the text, no paraphrase, no bracketed modifier. The note
names the readings by their grammatical labels, names the arguments, and names who holds them where
a name is available and cited. The note says plainly when the evidence does not settle the
question.

**It does not permit.** Cowardice — where the grammar settles the matter, the text follows it and
says so. Fabricated readings — a reading with no support in the grammar, made to serve a doctrine,
is not an ambiguity. Reopening what textual grounds have closed (6.5).

**How the text is chosen when the crux is real:** not by majority, not by tradition, not by the
translator's probability, which is the least checkable thing in the room.

1. **Print the rendering that forecloses the fewest live options.**
2. **Count words by function, not by occurrence.** Greek marks indefiniteness by the absence of the
   article and English by its presence, so the English article can be the exponent of a feature the
   source has.
3. **Prefer the rendering that keeps the source's part of speech.**
4. **No rendering may require later doctrinal apparatus to be understood.** 1.4 governs.

## 6.2 The bare predicate (John 1:1c)

*The grammar.* Three observations.

1. The previous clause names the Father and marks him with the article — "with the God".
2. The contested clause has no article on the predicate, while the subject carries its own. The
   writer had just shown he knew how to put an article on the word when he wanted to name the
   Father.
3. He made that change inside the same sentence. The bare predicate is the difference between the
   two clauses.

The absence marks the predicate as **non-identifying**: the Word is not being named as the one just
named. An English rendering that collapses the two clauses says less than the source says.

**Where the grammar stops.** It says the Word is what the predicate says. It does not say the Word
is a second deity, and it does not say the Word is the Father under another name. Any account that
fills that gap is doing work the sentence did not do. **Both overreadings are refused together** —
the high one that requires a distinction of persons so the English stops contradicting itself, and
the low one that takes a bare noun as a marker of a lesser being. They are one error in two
directions.

**The printing (Part 8, item 30).** The text prints **"and the Word was a god"**: an indefinite
noun rendered with an indefinite noun, which keeps the predicate a noun and keeps the distinction
the sentence makes. "Divine" converts the noun into an adjective and changes the kind of claim.
"The Word was God" collapses the two clauses. Both are refused.

**What the note must carry.** The definite reading with its strongest support, which is not this
verse but the anarthrous predicate at John 1:18. The indefinite reading beside the qualitative one,
with the honest admission that English "a god" can suggest one deity among several where the source
need not. The count-noun objection, with its weakness stated. The divine council background, with
the caution that availability is not intention. And John 5:18 and 20:28, so that one clause does
not carry a whole Christology.

**One clause is evidence for what is possible. A whole book is evidence for what is
characteristic.** The text prints the clause and puts the wider usage in the note, and neither
overrules the other.

## 6.3 The bare ἐγώ εἰμι (John 8:58)

*The proposal.* Translate the bare present as a present of past action: "I have been".

*Why it fails here.* That construction is defined by an **adverbial phrase of duration**, and does
not exist without one.

| Verse | Adverb | Type |
|---|---|---|
| John 14:9 | "so long a time" | duration — "I have been" is correct there |
| John 15:27 | "from the beginning" | duration — "you have been" is correct there |
| John 8:58 | "before Abraham came to be" | **boundary, not duration** |

A boundary clause sets a terminus ante quem. It does not measure a span running up to the present.
The two parallels most often cited for "I have been" are the two that show why it does not apply
here.

*What the verse says.* The contrast is between "come to be", said of Abraham, and "I am", said of
Jesus: a bounded event against a being unbounded by it. That is a claim about priority of
existence, stated in the tenses. Whether it is also a claim about the divine name the grammar does
not settle.

*The control.* Put any uncontested name in place of Abraham — *before that man came to be, I am*.
No translator renders that "I have been". **A reading that cannot survive the substitution is not a
grammatical reading**, and this is why interpretive capitals are banned: a capital is the one move
that does not survive the substitution.

*What stays open.* The echo of the divine name at the bush is not a quotation, since neither the
Hebrew nor the Greek there reads as this verse does. The nearer parallel is Psalms 90:2, "before
the mountains were brought forth ... you are", which shares the preposition, the verb and the bare
present, and which nobody treats as a quotation. And John 9:9 is the standing counter-example: the
man born blind answers with the same words, and no one reads him as claiming deity. That does not
settle 8:58. It shows the phrase is not a formula that automatically carries the divine name.

**The printing.** The text prints **"before Abraham was, I am"** — present tense, plain type, no
capital.

## 6.4 The shared-article construction

*The verses.* **Titus 2:13** and **2 Peter 1:1**. The ambiguity gate (7.1, gate 9) runs on those
two verses. The literature names the construction for a scholar. This Code does not name it for
anyone, because a scholar's name is not a law.

*The construction.* A single article governing two substantives joined by "and".

*What the rule claims.* As stated, it covers **personal, singular, common** substantives only.
Proper names and plurals fall outside it. Where those three conditions hold, the two substantives
share one referent.

*Where it is stretched.* The two verses for which it is most often invoked both have a proper name
as the second substantive, which is exactly the case the rule excludes. Applying it there is a
later extension, not the rule's own claim.

**The ruling.** The construction is a container. It does not determine identity, and in those
verses it does not fall inside the rule invoked for it. Both are printed under 6.1. The note records
the single-referent and the two-referent reading and states that the second substantive is a proper
name. This Part will not print "our great God and Savior" as though the grammar had established it,
and will not print a two-referent rendering as though it had refuted it. Both are the same failure
wearing different clothes.

## 6.5 What is not an ambiguity

| Crux | Ruling | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Habakkuk 1:12, "you shall not die" | Settled: documented correction, earlier reading printed | Law I |
| Psalms 22:16, *ka'ari* | Settled: Hebrew in the text, Greek in the note | 4.1, Rule 7 |
| Psalms 110:1, "my lord" | Settled: lower case, a human title | Part 8, item 5 |
| Psalms 45:6, "Your throne is God" | Printed as ruled — settled by instruction, not by grammar, and the note carries the range | Part 8, item 6 |
| Isaiah 7:14, "the young woman" | Settled: philology, not ambiguity | Law IV |
| 1 John 5:7–8, the Comma | Settled: an interpolation, excised | 5.3 |
| Mark 16:9–20 and John 7:53–8:11 | Settled: demarcated as later | 5.3 |

An ambiguity that the evidence closes is not kept open out of even-handedness. That is false
balance, which is its own kind of dishonesty.

# PART 7: THE AUDIT

## 7.1 The gates

**A volume is published only if every gate exits zero. A gate that crashes is a failed gate. A gate
that cannot run is a failed gate. A gate that passes because it checked nothing is the worst of the
three.** All of them fail closed.

1. **Quote.** Every note head quotes the volume's own text, in every layout the volume uses. Zero
   mismatches.
2. **Register.** Zero semicolons, zero negative inversions, zero "that" for persons, zero archaic
   pronouns and verbs in running text.
3. **Archaism.** The second register pass, with the anatomy carve-out as a tripwire. Every survivor
   documented.
4. **Anatomy.** No organ replaced by an abstraction.
5. **Structure.** Law VII layout, and the chapter and section counts against the source. The
   split-and-rejoin assertion equals the input.
6. **Presence.** Every verse present. Nothing dropped, merged or compressed.
7. **Provenance.** The volume contains its own book and no other: the verse set of each chapter
   matches the source, and each rendered verse shares its content words with the source at that
   coordinate.
8. **Reference.** Every scripture citation resolves to a verse that exists. A citation outside the
   canon being checked is reported as **unverifiable**, never as a pass.
9. **Ambiguity.** Every crux in Part 6 is printed with the literal rendering and a note carrying
   the full range.
10. **Direction.** Every reading adopted against the received text is justified by showing which
    reading the other was made from, not by the age of the manuscript.

## 7.2 The five failures

| # | Failure | Penalty |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | **Fabrication of form** — a verse, chapter, book or quotation that does not exist | Volume fails. Delete and rebuild the unit |
| F2 | **Fabrication of fact** — a false hapax, a misattributed scholar, a siglum on the wrong scroll, a wrong gloss | Volume fails. Strike the claim, and the note states what was struck |
| F3 | **Violation of a standing rule** — a banned rendering, a missing acrostic label, an interpretive capital | Volume fails. Restore the mandated form and log it |
| F4 | **Unverified self-report** — a count that does not match, a check claimed and not run | Volume fails. Recount and restate. A second occurrence fails the volume outright |
| F5 | **Claimed correction** — a statement that a defect was fixed when it is not fixed | Volume fails as F2. The volume is rebuilt from the corrected text |

**The ranking is by concealment, not by size.** F4 is the smallest error and the most serious,
because an inaccurate disclosure removes the reader's reason to check anything else.

## 7.3 The poison suite

**A gate that has never been seen to fail has not been seen at all.** Every gate is tested with at
least one input it must reject, at least one clean input it must still pass, and the case that
reproduces each failure in 7.2. **Half the suite is clean cases**, because a gate that fails
everything catches every poison and is useless. Break one gate deliberately, run the suite, and
confirm it names the broken gate. Test the tier, not the pipeline: call the gate directly.

**The gate list is not the law, and the law is not the gate list.** The Laws' mechanical coverage
is partial at best, and 7.4 governs what may be claimed about the rest.

## 7.4 What a pass is not

- The badge certifies the mechanical gates only. **A badged volume can still be wrong.**
- Compliance is not correctness. A volume that passes has no invented citations. It does not have
  no errors.
- A green audit is not a verdict on authorship (2.3).

**Some clauses have no detector and will never have one**, because "does not write to protect the
institution" is a judgment about stance, not a property of a string. **No number is printed for
those clauses here.** A count without the list behind it is a verification claim about a check that
was never run — the fourth failure in its most respectable costume. The audit output states what it
checked, and everything else is written down as unchecked.

**What may never be said,** each one an F4 unless the work was done and can be shown:

1. "The text is verified." Unless the publication gate returned zero in this session.
2. "I checked every verse." Unless the mechanism and its output can be named.
3. "This is a hapax" or "occurs only here." Unless the search was run and can be shown.
4. "X argues Y." Unless the place where X says it can be named.
5. "The witnesses agree." Unless the witnesses and their readings can be named.

**Replace each with what is true.** "The mechanical gates passed and these claims are unchecked" is
a sentence that may always be written.

## 7.5 The decision log

Every volume carries `books/<name>.decisions.md`, in five sections:

| Section | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Lexical | a term choice, what was rejected, and which rule decided it |
| Textual | a reading adopted or rejected, and the witness that decides it, or its absence |
| Syntax | how a clause was parsed, and the alternative rejected, for what reason |
| Register | an archaism retained as load-bearing, and why it is not a violation |
| Unresolved | a crux nobody can settle. "Retained for now" is honest. Silently deciding is not |

The log is mandatory because a volume must be rebuildable from its base plus its log, because an
unsettled crux must have somewhere to park, and because it is the paper trail the signature rests
on. **An empty Unresolved section on a hard text is more suspicious than one with three entries.**

## 7.6 Working where no code can run

A manual check is weaker than a mechanical one. The method still works, the failure modes change,
and the report says which mode it was in (5.2). **Everything here is a hand-over to the audit,
never a substitute: the moment a count reads as a pass, it violates 2.4, duty 12, and 2.3 with
it.**

**The manual quote check.** Quote four to eight words in a note head, never more. Write all notes
last. Never edit the text after writing a note that quotes it. Re-check by literal search. **Never
paraphrase inside a quotation mark.**

**The manual provenance check.** A model that has read one book and is now writing another will
produce the first while feeling exactly like the second. One book per conversation. State the book,
chapter and verse range at the top of every reply. List the verses before rendering and the verses
after, and treat any difference as a dropped or merged verse. Never hold two books' source text at
once. Work by chapter: a whole book in one pass is compressed, and compression is forbidden.

**The manual register check.** At the end of each chapter, search the running text for every item
below and report the count. Anything found is either a defect or a note quotation, and the report
says which.

```
semicolon   behold   thou  thee  thy  thine  ye
cometh goeth saith doth hath maketh taketh giveth knoweth seeth
begat begotten   fowl  beast  corn  ass  asses   slay  slew  slain   smite  smote  smitten
lest  save  for ever  loving-kindness  wherewithal  goodly  comely   damsel  maiden  maid
brethren  twain  abide  tarry  whatsoever  whoso   it came to pass   nigh  sore  grievous
compassed about  maid-servant  gotten  vantage  victuals  raiment   mote  beam
labour  honour  favour  defence  centre  armour  neighbour
```

Copy the list fresh from 4.1, Rule 4 before each chapter, so the two lists cannot drift apart.

**The manual negation test.** Search for "not" and "never" standing directly after a finite verb —
"walks not", "saith never". Each hit is rewritten.

**The manual relative test.** At every relative pronoun, persons take "who", things and fused
relatives take "that" (Part 8, item 28).

**The disclosure obligation, and it is exact.** At the end of every chapter in this mode:

> **Chapter N, verses N:N-N:M.** Source verses: **N**. Verses rendered: **N**. Notes written: **N**.
> Manual quote check: **run / not run**. Manual register check: **run, N hits, all accounted for /
> not run**. I could not mechanically diff this against the source and I am not claiming I did.

If you cannot count, say you cannot count. An invented verification is worse than an admitted
absence, because it removes the reader's reason to check.

**Hand it back.** A volume checked by hand is compliant-pending-verification and cannot be badged.

# PART 8: THE OPERATOR'S RULINGS

**Explicit rulings of the requester. Binding, and not revisable by inference.**

**Names and geography**

1. Personal and place names take their standard English forms, and the divine name is Jahve.
2. Israel stays English.
3. A name that exists only in Greek keeps the Greek form.

**Theological cruxes, as printed**

4. Psalms 22:16 — "like a lion, they are at my hands and my feet". The Hebrew in the text, the
   Greek reading in the note.
5. Psalms 110:1 — lower-case "my lord", a human title.
6. Psalms 45:6 — "Your throne is God forever and ever."
7. Psalms 82:1 — "God stands in the assembly of El."
8. Psalms 78:49 — "a mission of evil angels."

11. Psalms 51:5 — "in sin did my mother conceive me."

14. Psalms 137:9 prints in full, every word, and no euphemism or register rule may pass a softened
    rendering of it.

**The beatitudes**

15. "Blessed is the one who ..." as the standard form, except at Psalms 1:1, which keeps "the man".

**Format and punctuation**

16. "Selah." takes a period.

18. "Ezrahite" is retained as the gentilic in the psalm headings.
19. No editorial symbol — asterisk, dagger, star, dot — marks anything in a volume's running text.
    The acrostic letters are the one exception.

**Substance**

21. Nothing is dropped or compressed. Notes quote the volume's own wording.
22. No euphemism.
23. Do not adjudicate source criticism.
24. The tetragrammaton is Jahve.
25. The divine name is not imported into the New Testament.

**Register**

26. Modern English register: no archaic pronouns or verbs, no inverted negation, no British
    spellings, no semicolons.
27. "Behold" is banned outright.
28. "Who" for persons, "that" for things and fused relatives.
29. Push everything modern. We are not a museum. We are a forensic historical document.

**Printings fixed by instruction, outside the consistency rule (4.4, item 7)**

30. John 1:1 prints "and the Word was a god."
31. John 1:18 prints "the one and only god", both words lower case.
32. John 20:28 prints the definite.
