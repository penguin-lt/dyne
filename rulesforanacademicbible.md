# RULES FOR AN ACADEMIC BIBLE
## The consolidated code of the Dyne translation

*Canonical law of the project. One file, one order, no duplication. Where this file and any other
document differ, this file governs, except on the one point where it defers to the volume's own
specification (see 7.1a).*

**Consolidated:** 19 to 26 October 2026. This file is the project's only rule document. It states
every rule, what it forbids, and how a failure is detected. **Cite a Part by its title, not its
number. Numbers drift, titles do not.**

**What this file is not.** The reasoning behind a rule lives elsewhere. This file states the rule,
what it forbids, and how a failure is detected. A rule with no test is unfinished and is marked
as such.

# PART ONE: STATUS AND ORDER OF AUTHORITY

## 1.1 What this translation is

An academic edition for historical-critical study. It reports what the ancient authors wrote, in
ordinary modern English, without fear of the institution and without hatred of the believer.

Three standing descriptions govern every decision:

> **"Push everything modern. We are not a museum. We are a forensic historical document."**

> **"We do not sanitize the ancient physiology."**

> **"The text belongs to the reader's own sovereign judgment."**

## 1.2 Order of authority

1. **This file.** It contains the roles, the law, the register, the terminology, the format, the
   textual policy, the ambiguity rule, the audit, and the standing instructions.
2. **The Eight Laws** (Part Four) are supreme over everything except the Ambiguity Rule where a
   source text is genuinely ambiguous.
3. **The Ambiguity Rule** (Part Eight) is the only Part that can suspend a rendering mandated
   elsewhere in this file.
4. **The Jewish Mandate** (1.3) ranks with the Eight Laws wherever a ruling touches the
   interpretation of the sources.
5. **The standing instructions** (Part Ten) are preserved explicitly and cannot be overridden by
   inference from a general rule. Where one departs from a Law, the departure is deliberate.
6. **The terminology tables in Part Six** are the reference of first resort for the rendering of a
   single word. They are in this file and nowhere else.
7. **This file is self-contained.** There is no second rule document and no glossary beside it.

**No other document holds law.** A volume, a decision log and a specification block are outputs,
audited against this file. None of them may create a rule, and a rule remembered from a document
that no longer exists is not a rule.

## 1.3 The Jewish Mandate

**These are Jewish texts, and this translation is answerable to the world that produced them.**

1. **No sanitizing of the ancient physiology.** The organs of grief, the viscera, the genitals and
   the dung are rendered as the sources render them. Law II carries the detail.
2. **No nineteenth-century gentility.** Victorian propriety is not a translation principle.
3. **No fourth-century Greek philosophical categories imposed on first-century Jewish monotheism.**
   *Ousia* and *hypostasis* are later apparatus. They may appear in a note as reception history and
   may never be required to make a sentence of the text coherent.
4. **The Second Temple divine realm is reported, not flattened.** The divine council, the Angel of
   the Lord and the Logos are part of the world these texts inhabit. Law I governs the Hebrew side
   and the Ambiguity Rule applies it to the Greek.
5. **Where two renderings are equally defensible, prefer the one that can be read without later
   doctrinal apparatus.** A sentence that cannot be understood without a fourth-century creed has
   been translated into that creed.

**What the Mandate does not license.** It is not a mandate for a low Christology. It forbids a
translation from *requiring* later doctrine. It does not forbid the text from saying more than the
Mandate expected, and where the text does, the text wins.

## 1.4 The penalty clause

A translation that violates these laws is not a translation. It is a theological pamphlet or a
counterfeit. It is a counterfeit if it hides that God commanded the slaughter of nursing infants,
if it turns enslaved people into "bondservants", if it injects later dogma into first-century
Greek, if it invents permissive language to protect God from his own text, or if it softens the
viscera of grief into an abstraction.

## 1.5 House style, which binds this file too

No semicolons anywhere, in any file. No Victorian archaism. No British spellings. Where a note
quotes an older English version, the quotation keeps that version's wording and the note says so.

# PART TWO: THE TWO ROLES

## 2.1 Separation

**Every rule has one owner at the moment it runs. Where a rule must run at two points of the work,
it is two duties, one per phase, and the ownership table (3.4) names each duty and its owner.** A
translator works from the source toward a text. An auditor works from the finished text backward
toward the rules.

1. **Neither list is handed to the other role.** A translator given the audit criteria writes to
   the check instead of to the source, and produces a text that passes gates and reads like a
   submission. An auditor given the translator's aims grades the intention instead of the artifact.
2. **The split is by role, not by difficulty.** A rule goes to the auditor when the only way to
   know whether it was obeyed is to look at the finished text from outside. It stays with the
   translator when it can only be obeyed by the person making the sentence.
3. **The auditor does not translate and the translator does not audit.** One person may hold both
   roles in a project. They may not hold both on the same volume. The reviewer who signs a volume is
   never its author.
4. **This is law, not administration.** Where the two lists conflict on substance, the conflict is a
   defect in this Part and is resolved in 3.4, never by whichever role is speaking.

## 2.2 The order of operations

| Step | Role | In | Out |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. **Draft** | translator, human or model | the specification and the source | the running text and the notes, **and nothing else** |
| 2. **Audit** | a different pass | the draft, unchanged | a verdict per clause, from the audit's own output |
| 3. **Fix** | back to the translator | the audit's fail list | a revised draft |
| 4. **Re-audit** | step 2 again | every clause, not only the failed ones | a verdict per clause |
| 5. **Sign** | a reviewer who is not the author | the audited volume | a signature, which is not a check |

**A draft is not an audit and an audit is not a draft.** They fail in different ways, and a pass
that does both in one sitting has done neither. The audit is clause by clause, and the re-audit is
the whole clause list, because a fix for one Law can break another.

## 2.3 The authorship label is assigned after translation, by audit

**Whether a text was made by a person or a model is an audit question.** It is not a header, it is
not front matter, and it is not something asked of whoever is writing.

1. **The translator does not declare it.** Nothing written during drafting may state or imply
   whether the work is human or machine. A statement about its own process is a claim the process
   cannot observe, and it is the failure class the audit exists to catch.
2. **The label is assigned at step 2 or later, from evidence.** It records in three states, never
   two: **verified human**, **verified model**, or **undetermined**.
3. **Undetermined is the default, and it is not a failure.** An audit that cannot tell does not
   guess. Guessing human is flattery, guessing model is an accusation, and both are an unsupported
   claim standing where a verdict belongs.
4. **The label is not a quality claim.** Compliance does not depend on who or what made the text.
   A machine-made volume that passes is compliant. A human-made volume that fails is dead. There is
   no third case. **The badge checks the text, not the maker.**
5. **What counts as evidence.** Only what is in the artifact and can be named: a dated signature,
   a decision log in a named hand, a transcript, a version history, or a declaration from the
   operator. **An impression is not evidence.** "This reads like a model" is refused for the same
   reason as any other impression.
6. **It is recorded once**, in the volume's decision log, with the evidence beside it. If the
   evidence disappears, the label is withdrawn to **undetermined** in a dated line.

# PART THREE: THE TWO LISTS

**A translator reads 3.1 and 3.1a and stops. An auditor reads 3.2 and stops.** Reading the other role's list
bends the work in the wrong direction before a word is written.

## 3.1 The translator's list

**Fourteen duties, done to the source, before the text exists in finished form.** They run for
every book, and again for every chapter.

1. **Read the source as the source.** Establish the base text and its edition, and know which
   witnesses are in the room, before writing. Part Seven.
2. **Render the word, not the tradition of the word.** The terminology tables are statute. Where a
   table is silent, the word's range in the source decides, and the decision is logged once.
3. **Print the most literal English the grammar allows.** Where the grammar leaves more than one
   reading live and they carry different freight, do not choose: the text prints the literal
   rendering and the note carries the range. Part Eight.
4. **Run the substitution control.** Put an uncontested name in place of the contested one. If the
   rendering changes, it was doctrine and not grammar.
5. **Refuse the typography temptation.** Capitals, small caps, italics, scare quotes. 6.5, rule 7.
6. **Keep the anatomy an anatomy.** Organs are organs, victims are the victims of named agents, and
   the agent of a verb is the agent the source names. Laws II, III and V are obeyed in the act of
   writing the clause or not at all.
7. **Do not harmonize what the source left in pieces.** Where two voices disagree, both stand.
   Where a seam is visible, the seam is printed. Law VI.
8. **Nothing is sanitized.** Anatomy, violence, sexual violence, slavery, the cost to the victim.
   It prints.
9. **Use the names this Code mandates.** Standard English forms, the divine name as 6.1 states it,
   and the three reversals of 6.4. A form printed against 6.4 in either direction is a defect.
10. **Modern register as you write.** No semicolon, no "behold", no archaic verb, no inverted
    negation, "who" for persons and "that" for things. Part Five.
11. **Write every note head from the text as printed, never from the intention.** The head is
    written after its verse is final, and the audit re-derives it against the verse.
    Part Seven, 7.4.
12. **Disclose nothing about yourself.** No statement of origin, no ledger, no self-assessment, no
    claim that a rule was followed. This list contains no rule about verifying the translator's
    work. 2.3 governs.
13. **List the verses before rendering, and the verses after.** Give the audit both lists. The audit
    counts and compares them. The translator produces evidence and certifies nothing.
14. **Deliver the text and stop.** Anything the translator writes about the quality of its own
    output is discarded unread. Where a rule and the source disagree and the translator believes the
    rule is wrong, the translator says so and stops. It does not print against the table and
    explain in a note.

If one of these cannot be answered, say which and why, and let the operator decide. **Do not answer
it by assumption.**

**What the translator is not responsible for.** Proving the text is compliant, and knowing whether
it is a person or a model. Both belong to the audit.

## 3.1a Scope discipline, and the counting rule

**This section is work, not verdicts, and both roles run it: the translator before and while
writing, the auditor against the finished text.** **The commonest failure on this project was not an
error of fact. It was an error of scope** — a chapter produced for a book that was already finished,
which re-decided settled rulings, forked the verse numbering and duplicated notes.

1. **Before writing, check whether the book is already done.** If it is, read the existing version
   first. Its rulings bind.
2. **If a draft differs from what exists, stop and ask.** A change may be proposed and may not be
   made silently. Two versions of one book with different text is the worst outcome available, worse
   than any single bad rendering.
3. **If the operator names a book already complete, confirm the task.** A fresh independent edition,
   a revision, or a replacement are three different jobs with three different acceptance criteria.

**The counting rule.** Every number in a disclosure block is a **counted** number. Count the note
heads, the verses, the citations. If you cannot count, write "not counted" and say why. An estimated
number is an invented one.

## 3.2 The auditor's list

**Eleven things, done to a finished text from outside it.**

1. **Take the artifact unchanged.** A repair made during an audit voids the audit of that passage,
   because the thing audited is no longer the thing that was read.
2. **Run the mechanical gates first and record what they printed.** What a failed gate is, 9.1
   states once and this list does not repeat. A pass read from memory is not a pass.
3. **Give every clause one of four verdicts: PASS, FAIL, MANUAL, N/A.** MANUAL is not a pass. It
   means no detector exists and a person must read it, and it is the honest verdict for most of the
   Law list.
4. **Check the note heads against the rendered text, not against the intention.** Part Seven, 7.4.
5. **Check every factual claim in every note against a named source** — a hapax, an attribution, a
   siglum, a lexical gloss. None of these can be verified by reading the sentence that makes the
   claim, which is why they are on this list and not the translator's.
6. **Re-read every note for the claim that sounds certain and has never been checked.** A number is
   a claim. A superlative is a claim. "The volume prints X" is a claim about a file, and the file is
   right there. **Read the verse line before accepting a claim about what the verse says.**
7. **Check provenance, not only conformity.** A volume can pass every style gate while carrying
   another book's text. The verse set of each chapter must match the source's, and each
   rendered verse must share its content words with the source verse at that coordinate.
8. **Write the ledger from the audit's own output.** Not from memory and not from the translator's
   account. A ledger written by the pass it describes is a false claim about verification.
9. **Check statements of correction against the artifact.** Every claim that something was fixed
   names the file and the verse, so the next reader can confirm it in one action.
10. **Assign the authorship label from evidence, or decline to assign it.** 2.3.
11. **Report, and do not fix.** Corrections go back to the translator as a fail list, and the
    revised text comes back for a full re-audit.

**What the auditor may never do.** Accept a translator's self-report as evidence. Pass a clause
because the text reads well, because the author is reputable, or because a model would not have made
that mistake.

## 3.3 What binds both roles

| 2 | Modern register, no archaism, no inverted negation, no British spellings |

# PART FOUR: THE EIGHT LAWS

### Law I: Textual Pluriformity

**There is no single pristine original text.**

A translation does not privilege a late medieval manuscript over an earlier, superior witness. Where
the received text carries a later theological censorship, the text follows the older witness and the
received reading goes in the note.

**But age is not the test. The direction of the corruption is the test.** A reading is adopted
because it can be shown to be the reading the other was made from, not merely because a manuscript
is older. A documented scribal correction that softened the text is by definition later than the
reading it replaced, however old the manuscript that carries it.

| Case | Test | What is printed |
|---|---|---|
| **Censorship** | The softer reading introduces a doctrine, a dignity or a scruple the harder reading does not have, and there is no motive for the reverse change | The **harder, earlier** reading. The received text in the note |
| **Documented correction** | The tradition preserved both the reading and the fact that it changed it | The **earlier** reading at full strength. The received text in the note, named as a correction |
| **True variant** | Neither reading explains the other, and the difference is not doctrinal | The better-attested reading, the other in the note |

**Why the second case is not an exception.** It is the first case with better evidence. A scribal
correction is a censorship that the censors admitted.

**What the law does not do.** It does not license the reverse snobbery that treats every old
reading as early and every received reading as corrupt. That is a textual judgment, made case by
case and recorded in the note.

**Worked case (Habakkuk 1:12).** The received text reads *lo namut*, "we shall not die". The
tradition records *lo tamut*, "you shall not die", addressed to God, as the reading the scribes
replaced, because associating death with God was intolerable to them. The Greek version already reads "we shall not die", which shows the corruption was already in
place when the Greek was made: early, not original. Whether any older witness carries "you shall
not die" is a question the note answers with named witnesses or marks unconfirmed, because a siglum
is never attached to a scroll whose contents have not been checked (6.5, rule 5). The parallelism settles it: "from everlasting" is answered by "you shall not die",
while "we shall not die" introduces a new subject and breaks the couplet. The text prints *lo tamut*
and the note records the received reading.

### Law II: Unvarnished Anatomy

**The ancient writers did not have Victorian sensibilities. We do not sanitize the ancient
physiology.**

No organ is replaced by an abstraction, and no visceral Hebrew or Greek metaphor is psychologized.

| Hebrew | Rendering | Forbidden |
|---|---|---|
| מֵעַי (*mê'ay*) | **bowels** | "inner parts", "insides", "inmost being", "heart", "stomach" |
| כָּבֵד (*kāḇêḏ*) | **liver** | "soul", "vital force" |
| כְּלָיוֹת (*kəlayōt*) | **kidneys** | "reins", "heart" |
| חֲלָצַיִם (*ḥălāṣayim*) | **loins** | "waist" where the idiom is sexual or generative |
| בֶּטֶן (*béṭen*) | **belly / womb** | "stomach", "tummy" |
| מְרֵרָה (*mərērāh*) | **gall** | "bitterness" where the organ is named |
| טִפֻּחִים (*ṭippuḥîm*) | **dandled** | "nursed", "born healthy" |
| פָּתָה (*pātāh*) | **seduce / seduced** | any softening verb |
| רַגְלַיִם (*raglayim*) | **genitals** where the euphemism requires it | "feet" left opaque |
| פַּרְשְׁדֹנָה (*paršəḏōnāh*) | **fecal filth** | "the dagger came out behind" |

**The carve-out, in force.** "Bowels" is not an archaism in this translation. It is the chosen word
wherever the Hebrew names the organ, and the anatomy of grief is **exempt from every register
sweep**. "Entrails" is reserved for animals and is never substituted for the human organ.

*The test case.* Judges 3:22 states that the fecal filth burst from the wound. Sanitizing it into
"the dagger emerged behind" erases the author's deliberate humiliation of Moab's king.

### Law III: Direct Causation

**God is responsible for what the text says he does.**

No permissive language is inserted to protect God's reputation, and רָע / רָעָה (*rā'*, "evil") is
never softened to "disaster", "harm", "adversity" or "calamity" when God brings or creates it.
**Do not flinch on *rā'*. It is "evil."** This holds even where "evil" sounds harsh about God. That
harshness is the text.

If the text says God hardened a heart, the translation does not say he "allowed" it to harden. If it
says God incited a census, the translation does not substitute an adversary. **God is the agent of
his own violence.**

*Worked case.* Jonah 1:4 reads "hurled a great wind", not "sent out a great wind".

### Law IV: Philological Absolutism

**Words mean what they meant to their ancient authors, not what later dogma wants them to mean.**

- עַלְמָה (*'almah*) is "young woman of childbearing age", not "virgin". "Virgin" is בְּתוּלָה.
- נֶפֶשׁ (*nefesh*) is "life, throat, living being", not an immortal soul. Animals have it too.
- עֶבֶד / δοῦλος (*'ebed*, *doulos*) is **slave**, property, in chattel, market and household
  contexts. Never "bondservant".
- אֱמוּנָה is "faithfulness, steadfast loyalty", not cognitive belief.
- שְׁאוֹל, ᾅδης, γέεννα and Τάρταρος are four distinct realms. None is the medieval Hell.
- רוּחַ אֱלֹהִים at Genesis 1:2 is "a mighty wind" or "divine breath", not the Holy Spirit of
  fourth-century trinitarianism.

A translation that bends a word's definition to support a Trinity, a hierarchy, or a prosperity
gospel is guilty of fraud.

### Law V: The Bleeding Victim

**The text is translated from the perspective of the crushed, not the powerful.**

- חֵרֶם (*ḥērem*) is **total extermination, ritual slaughter under taboo**, not "devotion to the
  Lord".
- The Amalekite infants of 1 Samuel 15 are עוֹלֵל and יוֹנֵק, nursing babies at the breast, not
  "vessels of wrath".
- The enslaved girl of Leviticus 25 is אֲחֻזָּה, inherited human property, not a "servant".
- In 1 Kings 1:1–4, Abishag's requisition is coercive state kidnapping, not an "honored
  palliative nurse".
- In Exodus 21:20–21 the statute is printed with legal clarity: beating a slave to death carries no
  punishment if the victim lingers a day or two, "for he is his property".

No euphemism for torture, infant slaughter or sexual violence. No pious softening of barbaric
violence into "devotion", "consecration" or "discipline".

### Law VI: Preserved Contradiction

**The Bible contradicts itself, and the translation lets the contradiction stand.**

- 2 Samuel 24:1 says God incited the census. 1 Chronicles 21:1 says an adversary did. Both print
  raw.
- 2 Kings 24:8 gives a king as eighteen, 2 Chronicles 36:9 as eight. The discrepancy stands.
- The resurrection accounts are printed as four, never synchronized.
- The Gospels' irreconcilable christologies are preserved. The terrified, forsaken Jesus of Mark
  is never infected with the serene Logos of John.

The notes highlight the seams, the edits and the arguments between the biblical authors. **They do
not referee them.** **Do not adjudicate source criticism.** The documentary voices may be named as
descriptive categories in a note. The translator reports the seam and does not rule on it.

### Law VII: Literary Formatting

**Chapter and verse numbers were medieval inventions. The ancient authors wrote literature.**

- Poetry is set as stanzas, keeping the parallelism. Acrostics are labeled by their letters, every
  letter, in order.
- Narrative and dialogue are set as prose, with speeches standing out.
- Law collections are set as legal documents, with epigraphic damage preserved.
- Inscriptions keep their line numbers, which is epigraphic convention and not chapter division.
- Letters and treaties are set as block text.
- Verse numbers appear only in the note heads and appendices.

### Law VIII: Sovereign Objectivity

**Translation is done without fear of the institution and without hatred of the believer.**

It is not written to protect a church, a sect or a publishing house, and it is not written out of
petty rebellion. It does not insert "[other]" into a verse, or "Jehovah" into the Greek, or change a
verse to enforce male domination, and it does not put scare quotes around a biblical figure to
police the reader's belief.

**Council-anxiety cuts both ways.** A reading is not improved by being embarrassing to a later
council and not worsened by being convenient to one. Late is not wrong and early is not right. The
evidence decides.

# PART FIVE: THE REGISTER STANDARD

The ancient writers used the everyday speech of their own street. A translation that dresses that
speech in Victorian flannel is less honest, not more reverent, because it interposes a foreign
century between the reader and the text. **Register must never cost the text its shock.**

### Rule 1. Negative inversion is abolished

Post-verbal negation ("walks not", "you answer not") is not English. Use do-support: "does not
walk", "you do not answer". This applies to the indicative, the imperative and the participle alike.

*The perfect-tense trap.* "Do not have spoken" is ungrammatical. The repair is "have not spoken",
"had not believed". This defect once recurred mechanically across six volumes and is now a standing
test.

### Rule 2. "Who" for persons, "that" for things

A subject relative whose antecedent is a person takes "who". "That" survives for things and in fused
relatives over things ("all that he had"), where "who" is impossible.

### Rule 3. הִנֵּה is a deictic pointer

It is rendered by its function in the scene, never by "behold", which is **banned outright**. Sudden
action takes "suddenly" or "just then". A pointed-out object or person takes "and there was" or "and
look". A shock takes "Look!"

the mountains **were born**, a woman **gave birth**.

### Rule 5. What is not modernized

Ordinary modern vocabulary stays even where it is also biblical: "seed", "womb", "loins", "cattle",
"vine", "incense", "Sabbath", "Sheol", and every proper name. The standard targets Victorian syntax
and vocabulary, not the Bible's own conceptual world. The vocative "O" is retained, since the
Psalter is unreadable without it. **The anatomy carve-out of Law II overrides every sweep.**

### Rule 6. Note heads follow the text

Any change to the translation invalidates every note head that quotes it. Heads are re-derived from
the rendered text after every pass, never patched by paraphrase. A note that quotes a wording the
text no longer carries is a compliance failure, not an inconsistency.

### Rule 7. The received text in the text, the versions in the note

Where the received Hebrew and a daughter version differ, and the difference has been load-bearing
for later theology, the text prints the Hebrew and the note records the version. The rule exists
because the alternative has been practiced for centuries: a Greek reading is promoted to the main
text, a Jewish reading is demoted to a footnote, and the reader is never told which is which.
Psalms 22:16 is the test case. The Hebrew says *ka'ari*, "like a lion". The Greek says "they
pierced". The English tradition has printed the Greek as though it were the Hebrew. It is not
printed that way here.

## 6.4 Names are standard English

**Personal and place names take their standard English forms** — Jesus, Moses, David, Jacob, Peter,
Paul, Mary, Jerusalem, Egypt, Babylon, Canaan, Zion, Galilee. **The divine name is Jahve
throughout.** A Semitic form standing in running text is a defect. The three reversals below are the whole list
of exceptions.

Three reversals need context rather than a table:
- **Jacob** is the patriarch, and **James** is the apostle.
- **Judah** is the tribe, **Judea** the province, and **Judas** the man. Judas is the default in
  1 and 2 Maccabees.
- **Israel** stays English. It is the people's own self-designation and not a Latinization.

**What the rule does not touch.** Modern scholars, editors and publishers are cited under their own
names, since renaming them would break the bibliography. Era and derivative terms are not personal
names and stay: Jewish, Israelite, Pauline, Johannine, Maccabean, Hasmonean.

## 6.5 Style-level prohibitions

1. **No word that post-dates the sources** as a translation equivalent: *Trinity, incarnation,
   eucharist, sacrament, orthodox, catholic, purgatory, grace* in the later technical sense,
   *church* for assembly, *mass, liturgy*. These appear only in historical notes, marked as later
   categories.
2. **No interpretive paraphrase** introduced to fix theology. The strangeness of the sources is
   preserved and discussed, not smoothed.
3. **No semicolons**, in any volume or any project file.
4. **No British spellings.**
5. **No confirmed siglum on an unconfirmed manuscript.** If the contents cannot be confirmed, write
   "a manuscript of the Judean desert" and say it is unconfirmed. A real siglum on the wrong scroll is
   the worst defect in the catalogue, because every element of it is true except the relation.
6. **No bracketed modifier and no scare quote**, ever, on the ground that the reader needs steering.
7. **No interpretive typography.** No italic, small cap or enlarged letter may signal a reading the
   plain words do not carry. "I am" is never printed "I AM". The manuscripts have no capitals, so
   every capital in a modern translation is an editorial decision, and a lawful capital has exactly
   two uses: a proper name, and a fixed title the language treats as one — "the Word", "Lord" as
   address, "Messiah", "Rock", "the Adversary", "the one and only Son". A capital may never settle an
   open ambiguity — where 8.1 governs a word, the word prints lower case whatever a doctrine would
   prefer, which is why "god" prints lower case at John 1:18 and "son" lower case at Psalms 2:12 —
   and the same word in the same sense prints the same way in every verse, **unless a standing
   instruction fixes the printing** (Part Ten, items 30 to 32).

# PART SEVEN: TEXT AND APPARATUS

## 7.1 The base text

**This Code names no translation, edition, manuscript or scholar as authoritative.** The base
translation, the critical editions of the source, the comparison versions and the reference works are
declared once, in each volume's specification block, with edition and year. The specification is
part of the volume and is audited with it.

### 7.1a The specification block

**Fill this in and keep it at the top of the working file. If a field is blank the volume is not
ready to be written, and a blank field is a failure of the audit.**

```
JOB SPECIFICATION
  Volume:             <book, chapter range>
  Base translation:   <author, title, year, public domain in which jurisdiction>
  Textual base OT:    <edition, or the named manuscript>
  Textual base NT:    <edition>
  Witnesses:          <list, or "primary only">
  Lexicon Hebrew:     <edition>
  Lexicon Greek:      <edition>
  Grammar:            <edition>
  Note density:       <textual / crux / full apparatus>
  Appendix:           <yes, no, or which>
  Mode:               <code and file tools / conversation only>
  Register ceiling:   Part Five, vocative O retained, anatomy carve-out ON
```

**Four notes on the block.**

1. **When two reference works disagree on a contested gloss, the note names both and says so.** It
   does not silently take the one that suits the rendering.
2. **If the operator cannot consult a critical apparatus at all, the notes must say so.** A note
   that reports a variant without saying where it is attested is quoting somebody else's summary,
   and it is written as a report of a report.
3. **Note density is a choice, not a default.** Textual only, crux notes, or full apparatus. The
   reference standard is the full apparatus, and a thinner volume says so here.
4. **Mode is asked, never guessed.** An environment that cannot run a check says so rather than
   claim the check. 9.6 governs.

**What the Code does require of that declaration.**

1. **The base must be a literal, public-domain translation** of the book being delivered, and the
   revision must be disciplined and traceable, not a fresh paraphrase.
2. **Every verse must be re-checked** against the critical edition named in the specification, and
   changed wherever the base followed a received text or an outdated critical judgment.
3. **The comparison versions must be public domain in the operator's jurisdiction**, and each is
   named with its edition.
4. **A descriptive reference work is not an authority.** Commentaries, lexicons and encyclopedias are
   cited descriptively and never as a base.
5. **None of this is imported into the running text.** A version is cited in a note, never printed
   as though it were the source.

## 7.2 Textual-critical policy

1. **The tetragrammaton never appears in the New Testament, in any form.** No Greek manuscript has
   it there. "Jehovah" is barred everywhere, because Jahve is the name.
2. **The Comma Johanneum (1 John 5:7–8)** is excised from the primary text without apology, as a
   late Latin interpolation.
3. **The Longer Ending of Mark (16:9–20) and the Pericope Adulterae (John 7:53–8:11)** are
   demarcated in the text as later additions absent from the oldest witnesses.
4. **Scribal corrections such as the tiqqunei soferim** are documented in the notes where they occur
   (Genesis 18:22, Job 7:20, Zechariah 2:8, Habakkuk 1:12), with both readings and the direction of
   the change. **The earlier reading prints**, by Law I, second case — the received text goes in the
   note, named as a correction.
5. **No bracketed dogmatic modifiers, no scare quotes, no sectarian repair of the text.**
6. **Greek *kyrios* in the Maccabees is "the Lord".** This is a standing choice scoped to the Greek
   books, with the note that a lost Hebrew original may have carried the tetragrammaton behind it.

## 7.3 How a volume is built

The base translation is scaffolding, not a second source. It supplies the starting wording and the
verse order, and every reading is then re-derived from the source text. Where base and source
disagree the source wins. The base is an input to the first step, and the source is the authority
over every step after it.

1. Take the declared base and stabilize the structure.
2. Modernize the English to Part Five.
3. Re-check every verse against the declared critical edition.
4. Apply Part Six.
5. Write the notes.
6. Deliver as `books/<name>.md`.
7. Set the literary layout per Law VII.
8. Run the audit of Part Nine.

**A volume contains, in this order:** the title and a standing subtitle identifying it as a
modern-English revision of a literal base, for historical-critical study. The base-text block. An
introduction covering genre, date, language, provenience, text and witnesses, and register. The
translation. The notes. Sources, with licenses. An appendix where the book calls for one.

## 7.4 The notes

- **A note head carries the citation and a verbatim quotation of the text as printed:**
  `**12:3 "the exact words of the verse"**`. The quotation must match the rendered text character
  for character after normalization.
- **A note explains and never preaches.** It documents the variant, names the crux, reports what the
  tradition did with it, and leaves the verdict to the reader.
- **A note gives, in this order and only where there is something to say:** the text as printed, the
  witnesses where they differ and it matters, the versions where the reading bears on the verse, the
  alternative renderings and why they were taken or not, the word's range and its other uses, and the
  historical or liturgical setting. **The absence of a field means there is nothing to report.**
- **Uncertainty is flagged, never hidden** inside smooth English.
- **No apologetic rationalization, no dogmatic cross-reference, no "the church teaches".** A note
  reports a book's own theology as the ancient author's belief, never as counsel to the reader.
- **Where the text and a daughter version diverge on a load-bearing point**, the note records the
  version and the text keeps the source reading (Part Five, Rule 7).

## 7.5 The text itself

- No chapter or verse numbers in the running text. They appear only in the note heads and appendices.
- Prose in continuous paragraphs, speeches standing out. Poetry in stanzas, keeping the parallelism.
- A superscription's numbering convention is stated once per volume and assumed thereafter.

# PART EIGHT: THE AMBIGUITY RULE

**Where the source grammar permits more than one reading, the text does not decide it.**

## 8.1 The rule

Where the grammar leaves more than one reading live and the readings carry different theological
freight, **the translation prints the most literal English the grammar allows and the note carries
the full range with the grammatical argument for each.** The translator does not declare a winner in
the text. **A printing rule is not a verdict:** a ruling that fixes how a verse prints decides
nothing about which reading is true, and the note carries the range even where the printing is fixed
by instruction.

**Why.** A translator who resolves a genuine ambiguity in the direction his own tradition prefers is
doing what the sectarian translator does. The only difference is which institution is served.

**What it requires.**
1. The literal rendering goes in the text, with no paraphrase and no bracketed modifier.
2. The note names the possibilities by their grammatical labels, names the arguments, and names who
   holds them where a name is available and cited.
3. The note says plainly when the evidence does not settle the question. "Most grammarians read this
   as definite" reports a majority, it does not demonstrate one.

**What it does not permit.**
1. **It is not a license for cowardice.** Where the grammar settles the matter, the text follows it
   and says so. Even-handedness in the face of settled evidence is a different failure.
2. **It does not cover fabricated readings.** A reading with no support in the grammar at all, made
   to serve a doctrine, is not an ambiguity. It is a fabrication.
3. **It does not reopen what textual grounds have closed** (see 8.5).

**How the text is chosen when the crux is real:** not by majority, not by tradition, not by the
translator's probability, which is the least checkable thing in the room. The criteria:

1. **Print the rendering that forecloses the fewest live options.**
2. **Count words by function, not by occurrence.** A word is not "added" when it does work the
   source marks by other means. Greek marks indefiniteness by the absence of the article and English
   by its presence, so the English article is the exponent of a feature the source has, not an
   importation. The question is always what a word is doing, never how many of them there are.
3. **Prefer the rendering that keeps the source's part of speech.** Changing a noun into an
   adjective alters the claim the sentence makes about its subject.
4. **No rendering may require later doctrinal apparatus to be understood.** 1.3 governs.

## 8.2 Application: the bare predicate (John 1:1c)

*The grammar.* Three observations.

1. **The previous clause names the Father and marks him with the article.** "With the God." The
   article is present and points to the one already named.
2. **The contested clause has no article.** The predicate is bare, and the subject carries its own
   article. The writer had just shown he knew how to put an article on the word when he wanted to
   name the Father.
3. **He made that change inside the same sentence.** The bare predicate is not an accident to be
   explained away. It is the difference between the two clauses.

**What the absence does.** It marks the predicate as **non-identifying**. The Word is not being
named as the one just named. An English rendering that collapses the two clauses into one predicate
says less than the source says.

**Where the grammar stops.** It says the Word is what the predicate says. It does not say the Word is
a second deity beside the Father, and it does not say the Word is the Father under another name. It
does not use a word for the divine nature, because the source has no such word here to use. Any
account that fills that gap is doing work the sentence did not do.

**Both overreadings are refused together.** The high overreading takes the bare predicate as the
full later creed and requires the reader to supply a distinction of persons so the English stops
contradicting itself. The low overreading takes a bare noun as a marker of a lesser being and turns a
statement about predication into a statement about the furniture of the universe. These are one
error in two directions.

**The ruling.** The text prints **"and the Word was a god."** The grounds are about English and not
about the source: an indefinite noun is rendered with an indefinite noun, which keeps the predicate a
noun and keeps the distinction the sentence makes. "Divine" preserves the distinction but converts
the noun into an adjective and changes the kind of claim being made. "The Word was God" collapses the
two clauses and cannot be read without apparatus the text does not contain. Both are refused.

*What the note must carry.* The definite reading with its strongest support, which is not this verse
but the anarthrous predicate at John 1:18. The indefinite reading beside the qualitative one,
with the honest admission that English "a god" can suggest one deity among several where the source
need not. The count-noun objection, with its weakness stated. The divine council background, with the
caution that availability is not intention. And John 5:18 and 20:28, so that one clause does not
carry a whole Christology.

**One clause is evidence for what is possible. A whole book is evidence for what is characteristic.**
The text prints the clause under the Ambiguity Rule and puts the wider usage in the note, and neither
overrules the other.

## 8.3 Application: the bare ἐγώ εἰμι (John 8:58)

The formula occurs both with a predicate and bare. The bare form is contested.

*The proposal.* Translate the bare present as a present of past action in progress: "I have been."

*Why it fails here.* The present of past action is a real construction, and it is defined by an
**adverbial phrase of duration**. It does not exist without one.

| Verse | Adverb | Type |
|---|---|---|
| John 14:9 | "so long a time" | duration, correctly rendered "I have been" |
| John 15:27 | "from the beginning" | duration, correctly rendered "you have been" |
| John 8:58 | "before Abraham came to be" | **boundary, not duration** |

A boundary clause sets a terminus ante quem. It says something is so on the near side of a boundary.
It does not measure a span running up to the present. **The two parallels most often cited for "I
have been" are the two that show why it does not apply here.**

*What the verse does say.* The contrast is between "come to be", said of Abraham, and "I am", said of
Jesus. The aorist infinitive marks Abraham's coming into being as a bounded event. The present marks
Jesus's being as unbounded by that event. That is a claim about priority of existence, stated in the
tenses. Whether it is also a claim about the divine name is a separate question and the grammar does
not settle it.

*What remains open.* The proposal is weaker, not worthless. A bare present before a past boundary
clause is not ordinary narrative and the awkwardness a reader feels is real. The text prints the
present, the note names the difficulty, and the judgment stays with the reader.

**The control, which is the honest test.** Ask what the same construction means where its subject is
not a contested figure. Put any uncontested name in place of Abraham: *before that man came to be, I
am*. No translator renders that "I have been". The incentive to soften is gone. **A reading that cannot survive the substitution is not a
grammatical reading**, and this is why interpretive capitals are banned (6.5, rule 7): a capital is the one
move that does not survive the substitution.

*What the verse does not settle.* Whether the bare phrase echoes the divine name at the bush. The
echo is not a quotation, since neither the Hebrew nor the Greek version there reads as this verse
does. The nearer parallel is Psalms 90:2, "before the mountains were brought forth ... you are",
which shares the preposition, the verb of coming-to-be and the bare present, and which nobody treats
as a quotation of anything. And whether the bare phrase functions as a title anywhere in this book at
all: **John 9:9 is the standing counter-example**, where the man born blind answers with the same
words and no one reads him as claiming deity. That does not settle 8:58. It shows the phrase is not a
technical formula that automatically carries the divine name.

**The ruling.** The text prints **"before Abraham was, I am"**, present tense, plain type, no capital.

## 8.4 Application: the shared-article construction

*The verses.* The construction is audited where it occurs and matters: **Titus 2:13** and
**2 Peter 1:1**. The ambiguity gate (9.1, gate 12) runs on those two verses. The literature names the construction for a scholar. This
Code does not name it for anyone, because a scholar's name is not a law. The published exchange
between the construction's defenders and its critics is the reading on this point.

*The construction.* A single article governing two substantives joined by "and": article,
substantive, "and", substantive.

*What the rule claims.* As stated, it covers **personal, singular, common** substantives
only. Proper names and plurals fall outside it. Where those three conditions hold, the two substantives
share one referent.

*Where it is stretched.* The two verses for which it is most often invoked both have a **proper name
as the second substantive**, which is exactly the case the rule excluded. Applying it there is a
later extension, not the rule's own claim, and the published exchange between its defenders and
its critics is where the reader looks.

*The ruling.* The construction is a container. It does not determine identity, and in those verses it
does not fall inside the rule invoked for it. Both are printed under 8.1. The text gives the most
literal rendering that does not force a decision. The note records the single-referent and the
two-referent reading and states that the second substantive is a proper name.

*What this Part will not do.* It will not print "our great God and Savior" as though the grammar had
established it, and it will not print a two-referent rendering as though it had refuted it. Both are
the same failure wearing different clothes.

## 8.5 What is not an ambiguity

| Crux | Ruling | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Habakkuk 1:12, "you shall not die" | Settled: documented correction, earlier reading printed | Law I |
| Psalms 22:16, *ka'ari* | Settled: Hebrew in the text, Greek in the note | Part Five, Rule 7 |
| Psalms 110:1, "my lord" | Settled: lower case, a human title | Part Ten, item 5 |
| Psalms 45:6, "Your throne is God" | Printed as ruled | Part Ten, item 6 — settled by instruction, not by grammar, and the note carries the range |
| Isaiah 7:14, "the young woman" | Settled: philology, not ambiguity | Law IV |
| 1 John 5:7–8, the Comma | Settled: an interpolation, excised | 7.2 |
| Mark 16:9–20 and John 7:53–8:11 | Settled: demarcated as later | 7.2 |

An ambiguity that the evidence closes is not kept open out of even-handedness. That is false
balance, which is its own kind of dishonesty.

# PART NINE: THE AUDIT

**This Part is the auditor's.** A translator may run a gate as a working aid and may not record its
result as an audit of the draft, because a pass obtained by the pass that wrote the text certifies
nothing.

## 9.1 The gates

| Gate | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Quote** | Does every note head quote the volume's own text, in every layout the volume uses |
| **Register** | Part Five: negation, relatives, lexemes, idioms |
| **Archaism** | The second register pass, with the anatomy carve-out as a tripwire |
| **Structure** | Law VII, and the chapter and section counts against the source |
| **Provenance** | Does the volume contain its own book and no other, and is every rendered verse on the page |
| **Reference** | Does every scripture citation resolve to a verse that exists |
| **Claims** | Every superlative and exclusivity claim, checked against the corpus |

**One command, and it is not optional.** A volume is published only if the publication gate exits
zero. **A gate that crashes is a failed gate. A gate that cannot run is a failed gate. A gate that
passes because it checked nothing is the worst of the three.** All three fail closed.

**The gates a volume must pass:**

1. Zero quote mismatches across the volume.
2. Zero semicolons.
3. Zero negative inversions.
4. Zero "that" for persons.
5. Zero Victorian archaisms in running text, with every survivor documented.
6. Chapter and section counts matching the source.
7. Every verse present. Nothing dropped, merged or compressed.
8. A structural assertion that any split and rejoin equals the input.
9. **The anatomy gate.** No organ replaced by an abstraction.
10. **The provenance gate**, both halves: the verse set of each chapter matches the source, and each
    rendered verse shares its content words with the source at that coordinate.
11. **The reference gate.** A citation to a book outside the canon being checked is reported as
    **unverifiable**, never as a pass. This gate proves a verse exists and proves nothing about
    whether the note describes it correctly.
12. **The ambiguity gate.** Every crux in Part Eight is printed with the literal rendering and a note
    carrying the full range.
13. **The direction gate.** Every reading adopted against the received text is justified by showing
    which reading the other was made from, not by the age of the manuscript.

## 9.1a The poison suite

**A gate that has never been seen to fail has not been seen at all.** Every gate is tested with at
least one input it must reject, at least one clean input it must still pass, and the case that
reproduces each failure in 9.2. **Half the suite is clean cases**, because a gate that fails
everything catches every poison and is useless.

**It must be seen to fail.** Break one gate deliberately, run the suite, and confirm the suite names
the broken gate. A suite that has never failed is a suite that cannot fail.

**Test the tier, not the pipeline.** A fixture built to test one rule will fail five unrelated gates,
so testing it end to end tests the fixture. Call the gate directly.

**The gate list is not the law, and the law is not the gate list.** The Laws' mechanical coverage
is partial at best, and 9.3 governs what may be claimed about the rest. A green suite means the
machine found nothing. It does not mean the volume is right.

## 9.2 The five failures

| # | Failure | Penalty |
|---|---|---|
| **F1** | **Fabrication of form** — a verse, chapter, book or quotation that does not exist | Volume fails. Delete and rebuild the unit |
| **F2** | **Fabrication of fact** — a false hapax, a misattributed scholar, a siglum on the wrong scroll, a wrong gloss | Volume fails. Strike the claim, and the note states what was struck |
| **F3** | **Violation of a standing rule** — a banned rendering, a missing acrostic label, an interpretive capital | Volume fails. Restore the mandated form and log it |
| **F4** | **Unverified self-report** — a count that does not match, a check claimed and not run | Volume fails. Recount and restate. A second occurrence fails the volume outright |
| **F5** | **Claimed correction** — a statement that a defect was fixed when it is not fixed | Volume fails as F2. The volume is rebuilt from the corrected text |

**The ranking is by concealment, not by size.** F4 is the smallest error and the most serious,
because an inaccurate disclosure removes the reader's reason to check anything else.

## 9.3 What is never reported as a pass

**Some clauses have no detector, and no amount of engineering will give them one**, because "does
not write to protect the institution" is a judgment about stance, not a property of a string. An
audit that printed PASS for those would be making the exact false claim this Part exists to prevent.

**No number is printed for those clauses here, and no list of them is kept in this file.** A count
without the list behind it is a verification claim about a check that was never run — the fourth
failure (9.2) in its most respectable costume. The audit output states what it checked, and
everything else is written down as unchecked.

**The audit tells you what the machine found. It does not tell you the volume is right**, and a
badge without that sentence beside it is making a claim the project cannot support.

## 9.4 What a pass is not

- **The badge certifies the mechanical gates only.** It does not certify the notes, the citations no
  gate reads, or the judgment calls. **A badged volume can still be wrong.**
- **Compliance is not correctness.** A volume that passes has no invented citations. It does not have
  no errors.
- **A green audit is not a verdict on authorship.** 2.3.

## 9.5 What may never be said

Each of these is an F4 unless the work behind it was done and can be shown.

1. "The text is verified." Unless the publication gate returned zero in this session.
2. "I checked every verse." Unless the mechanism and its output can be named.
3. "This is a hapax" or "occurs only here." Unless the search was run and can be shown.
4. "X argues Y." Unless the place where X says it can be named.
5. "The witnesses agree." Unless the witnesses and their readings can be named.

**Replace each with what is true.** "The mechanical gates passed and these four claims are unchecked"
is a sentence that may always be written.

## 9.5a The decision log, which every volume carries

**A ledger records what was disclosed. A decision log records what was decided**, and the second is
what an auditor is actually checking, because two translators can disclose the same ledger and have
made opposite choices. Every volume carries `books/<name>.decisions.md`, with five sections:

| Section | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| **Lexical** | a term choice, what was rejected, and which rule decided it |
| **Textual** | a reading adopted or rejected, and the witness that decides it, or its absence |
| **Syntax** | how a clause was parsed, and the alternative rejected, for what reason |
| **Register** | an archaism retained as load-bearing, and why it is not a violation |
| **Unresolved** | a crux nobody can settle. "Retained for now" is honest. Silently deciding is not |

**The log is mandatory for three reasons.** A volume must be rebuildable from its base plus its
log. An unsettled crux must have somewhere to park, because settling silently is the one forbidden
thing. And the log is the paper trail the signature rests on. **An empty Unresolved section on a
hard text is more suspicious than one with three entries.**

## 9.6 Working where no code can run

**A procedure is not a test, and a manual check is weaker than a mechanical one.** Where the
environment cannot execute a gate, the method still works, the failure modes change, and the report
says which mode it was in (7.1a). **Everything here is a hand-over to the audit, never a substitute:
the moment a count reads as a pass, it violates 3.1, rule 8, and 2.3 with it.**

**The manual quote check.** Quote four to eight words of a verse in a note head, never more, because
long quotations are the ones that drift. Write all notes last. Never edit the text after writing a
note that quotes it. Re-check by literal search of the text as printed. **Never paraphrase inside a
quotation mark.**

**The manual provenance check.** A model that has read one book and is now writing another will
produce the first while feeling exactly like the second. One book per conversation. State the book,
chapter and verse range at the top of every reply. List the verses about to be rendered, then the
ones actually produced, and treat any difference as a dropped or merged verse. Never hold two books'
source text at once. Work by chapter: a whole book in one pass is compressed, and compression is
forbidden.

**The manual register check.** At the end of each chapter, search the running text for every item
below and report the count. Anything found is either a defect or a note quotation, and the report
says which, item by item.

```
semicolon   behold   thou  thee  thy  thine  ye
cometh goeth saith doth hath maketh taketh giveth knoweth seeth
begat begotten   fowl  beast  corn  ass  asses   slay  slew  slain   smite  smote  smitten
lest  save  for ever  loving-kindness  wherewithal  goodly  comely   damsel  maiden  maid
brethren  twain  abide  tarry  whatsoever  whoso   it came to pass   nigh  sore  grievous
compassed about  maid-servant  gotten  vantage  victuals  raiment   mote  beam
labour  honour  favour  defence  centre  armour  neighbour
```

This is the left-hand column of Part Five, Rule 4, plus the pronouns and the archaic verb forms.
Copy it fresh from Rule 4 before each chapter, so the two lists cannot drift apart.

**The manual negation test.** Part Five, Rule 1 abolishes inverted negation. Search for "not" and
"never" standing directly after a finite verb — "walks not", "saith never". Each hit is rewritten to
the normal order, and the count goes in the disclosure.

**The manual relative test.** At every relative pronoun, ask whether the antecedent is a person or a
thing. Persons take "who", things and fused relatives take "that" (Part Ten, item 28).

**The disclosure obligation, and it is exact.** At the end of every chapter in this mode:

> **Chapter N, verses N:N-N:M.** Source verses: **N**. Verses rendered: **N**. Notes written: **N**.
> Manual quote check: **run / not run**. Manual register check: **run, N hits, all accounted for /
> not run**. I could not mechanically diff this against the source and I am not claiming I did.

**If you cannot count, say you cannot count.** An invented verification is worse than an admitted
absence, because it removes the reader's reason to check.

**Hand it back for the real gate.** When a volume is delivered in this mode, say plainly that the
check was manual and that a mechanical pass is owed. **A volume checked by hand is
compliant-pending-verification and cannot be badged.**

# PART TEN: STANDING INSTRUCTIONS

**Explicit rulings of the requester. Binding, and not revisable by inference.**

**Names and geography**
1. Personal and place names take their standard English forms, and the divine name is Jahve.
2. Israel stays English.
3. A name that exists only in Greek keeps the Greek form. There is no Semitic original to restore.

**Theological cruxes, as printed**
4. Psalms 22:16 — "like a lion, they are at my hands and my feet". The Hebrew in the text, the
   Greek reading in the note.
5. Psalms 110:1 — lower-case "my lord", a human title.
6. Psalms 45:6 — "Your throne is God forever and ever."
7. Psalms 82:1 — "God stands in the assembly of El."
8. Psalms 78:49 — "a mission of evil angels."
9. Psalms 29:1 — "you sons of gods."
10. Psalms 2:12 — lower-case "son."
11. Psalms 51:5 — "in sin did my mother conceive me."
12. Isaiah 7:14 — "the young woman", with the Greek in the note.
13. **Do not flinch on *rā'*. It is "evil."**
14. Psalms 137:9 prints in full, every word, and no euphemism or register rule may pass a softened
    rendering of it. The old verdict of flawless is history. The rule is the printing, and the test
    is the verse against Law II.

**The beatitudes**
15. "Blessed is the one who ..." as the standard form, with the exception at Psalms 1:1, which
    keeps "the man".

**Format and punctuation**
16. "Selah." takes a period.
17. The acrostic letters are labeled by name, and the Lamentations volume spells them *het* and
    *tet*.
18. "Ezrahite" is retained as the gentilic in the psalm headings.
19. No editorial symbol — asterisk, dagger, star, dot — marks anything in a volume's running text.
    The acrostic letters are the one exception, and item 17 governs them.
20. No semicolons.

**Substance**
21. Nothing is dropped or compressed. Notes quote the volume's own wording.
22. No euphemism.
23. Do not adjudicate source criticism.
24. The tetragrammaton is Jahve.
25. The divine name is not imported into the New Testament.

**Register**
26. Modern English register: no archaic pronouns or verbs, no inverted negation, no British
    spellings, no semicolons.
27. "Behold" is banned outright.
28. "Who" for persons, "that" for things and fused relatives.
29. Push everything modern. We are not a museum. We are a forensic historical document.

**Printings fixed by instruction, and so outside the consistency rule (6.5, rule 7)**
30. John 1:1 prints "and the Word was a god."
31. John 1:18 prints "the one and only god", both words lower case.
32. John 20:28 prints the definite.

# PART ELEVEN: OPEN ITEMS

**Everything unresolved in this file, stated here rather than buried.**

1. **The base text is not named in the Code.** 7.1 makes each volume declare it. If the operator
   wants one base fixed for the whole work, that is a decision, not an inference, and it belongs in
   Part Ten.
2. **The rules at 8.2 and 8.3 are not open.** They fix how such verses print and what the note must
   carry. What stays open is the reading, and one cross-verse set: John 1:1 prints the indefinite by
   instruction 30, while John 1:18 and 20:28 print the definite by instructions 31 and 32. The
   printings are fixed and the readings are not. The note at each of the three carries the full
   range, and the three verses are audited as one set, not verse by verse.
3. **Which clauses have no detector is not maintained in this file.** 9.3 states the principle and
   refuses to invent coverage. The per-clause list belongs to the audit output for each volume,
   because a list kept here would drift from whatever tool is doing the checking.
4. **A rule that names a source of error and gives no test for it is unfinished.** That is the
   standing test for every sentence in this file, and it applies to this file.
